## Strategic lessons captured this session

1. **My own gap analyses had real errors.** I claimed "an LLM judging its own subagents shares a system prompt" and "claude-master can't run PS 5.1" — both wrong on codebase verification. The user's "are both agents sure of their verdict" challenge forced a deep dive that exposed two categories of mis-claim: (a) confused capability vs protocol-prescription gaps, (b) under-grounded architectural proposals that don't respect the verified two-level topology. **Lesson: every architectural critique that names "structural bias" or "architectural fix" must be tested against the actual dispatch topology in the codebase BEFORE being floated. Generic "judge-jury-executioner" framing isn't enough.**

2. **30 → 32 gaps: arithmetic errors in synthesis tables persist even after explicit counting.** During Step 4 (propose approaches), my Family-coverage breakdown summed to 21/30 but the per-family Yes/No actually totaled 14/32. Self-review at Step 7 caught this. **Lesson: tabular counts must be re-summed during self-review; don't trust earlier-turn arithmetic as authoritative.**

3. **The brainstorming skill's "ask one question at a time" discipline was load-bearing.** Two clarifying questions (ambition + skill-body treatment) shaped the whole 720-line spec. Without those gates, the spec would have either over-shot (Approach 3) or under-shot (Approach 2) what the user actually wanted. **Lesson: when the user signals "deep work," resist the temptation to skip clarifying questions for the sake of speed.**
